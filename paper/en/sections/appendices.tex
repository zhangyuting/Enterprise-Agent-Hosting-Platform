\section{Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

Terms are listed in roughly the order they first appear; first-time readers may find it useful to read the whole list once.

\rowcolors{2}{SoftGray}{white}
\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\bfseries}L{3.9cm}L{\dimexpr\textwidth-3.9cm-2\tabcolsep\relax}@{}}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{PrimaryLight}
  \textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
  \midrule
  \endfirsthead
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{PrimaryLight}
  \textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
  \midrule
  \endhead
  Agent & A versionable software unit that can be given an identity and that, within one run, chooses models or tools from its input and produces a result. Not a model and not a prompt.\\
  Controllable / meterable / observable / auditable & The platform's four target properties: it can limit what an agent does, account for what each run cost, see whether the system is healthy, and replay who did what and when.\\
  Control plane / execution plane & The decision-and-record part the enterprise owns (identity, authorization, tasks, the ledger) versus the replaceable compute part where agents actually run.\\
  Harness & The general execution loop implemented by the platform, which calls models and orchestrates tools. All Q\&A agents run on it.\\
  T0 / T1 / T2 / T3 & The four kinds of agent, divided by who decides the next step: Q\&A (``answer from the script'', the platform's fixed loop), workflow (``follow the form'', a workflow drawn in advance), code (``bring your own program'', the tenant's own code), autonomous (``let the expert work'', a ready-made autonomous agent).\\
  Capability manifest & The declaration submitted when an agent is registered: values on the four axes, a streaming flag, a maximum run duration and the harness version.\\
  Four axes & The four independent dimensions of the manifest: loop owner, isolation, side-effect scope, recovery.\\
  Conformance certification & The mechanism by which a declared capability takes effect only after destructive tests (recovery after a process kill, rebuild after session reclaim, egress blocking).\\
  L1 / L2 / L3 & The three interface layers: the public unified API; the minimal contract between platform and container; the service exits through which containers reach enterprise capabilities.\\
  Run & One execution with a start, a terminal state, a budget and the ability to be canceled; the unit of metering and audit.\\
  Thread & A long-lived conversation history that may span many sessions and runs.\\
  Session & A short-lived lease on an isolated execution environment; occupies a machine while active and may be released when idle.\\
  Checkpoint & A safe point saved during a run, from which execution can continue after a failure instead of restarting.\\
  Interrupt and resume & When an agent must wait mid-run (for approval, for a callback), the platform saves its state, releases the machine, and continues from the archive when the result arrives.\\
  Action channel & One of the five paths through which a running agent can affect the outside world: being called, calling a model, calling a tool, asking a person, calling another agent.\\
  Idempotency & Repeating an operation produces no additional effect. The platform has creation idempotency (a retry does not create several runs) and side-effect idempotency (a retry does not perform a real action twice).\\
  Circuit breaker & Hard ceilings on a run's steps, calls, model usage, duration and spend; exceeding any one terminates the run.\\
  Effectively once & The guarantee composed from a checkpoint before each effect, the tool gateway's idempotency ledger and archived results: a real-world action is not repeated by a retry.\\
  Three-stage kill switch & Stop issuing tokens, have the gateways reject the agent, destroy the instance; the first two stages do not depend on the cloud vendor.\\
  On-behalf-of (OBO) delegation & A business system calling on behalf of an end user; ``on whose behalf'' must be a token issued by the identity provider.\\
  Identity provider (IdP) & The enterprise's central login and identity-issuing system, such as a single sign-on service.\\
  Platform token / run token & The two token classes: the first for people, CI and business services against the unified API; the second for running containers against the model and tool exits, bound to one run and valid for minutes.\\
  Permission-intersection rule & Effective permission is the intersection of the manifest, the tool grants and the initiator's own permissions, minus structural denials; any party can only narrow, never widen.\\
  Structural denials & Operations never available to agent principals; at the identity layer there is no path by which they can be granted.\\
  Credential vault & The central store for tool secrets: people may write, rotate and revoke but not read; only the tool gateway may fetch, briefly.\\
  Model gateway / tool gateway & The single exits for all model calls and all tool calls respectively, and the places where the platform checks, records and refuses.\\
  Sandbox & An isolated execution environment destroyed after use, for environment-interaction tools such as running code or driving a browser.\\
  Unified ledger & The enterprise-held, append-only record of events that serves the user interface, audit and metering together.\\
  Hash chain & Each event includes the hash of the previous one; altering any historical event breaks verification.\\
  Three books & The internal allocation book, the vendor book and the reconciliation book.\\
  Cloud adapter & The anti-corruption layer that translates the platform's five primitives (deploy, invoke, cancel, status, destroy) into vendor APIs.\\
  Kernel / adapter packs / policy packs & What stays fixed across enterprises; what is replaced per enterprise and vendor; what varies by industry and risk appetite.\\
  OpenTelemetry & The open standard for observability data---instrumentation, data format and transport; adopting it makes switching monitoring backends a change of destination address.\\
  MCP & The Model Context Protocol, an open standard for connecting tools to large-model applications.\\
  \bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Ten-Invariant Acceptance Checklist}
\label{app:checklist}

\rowcolors{2}{SoftGray}{white}
\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\bfseries}C{0.8cm}L{\dimexpr\textwidth-2.4cm-4\tabcolsep\relax}C{1.6cm}@{}}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{PrimaryLight}
  \textbf{\#} & \textbf{Acceptance item} & \textbf{Status}\\
  \midrule
  \endfirsthead
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{PrimaryLight}
  \textbf{\#} & \textbf{Acceptance item} & \textbf{Status}\\
  \midrule
  \endhead
  1 & No long-lived secret exists in containers, images, logs or snapshots & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  2 & Direct calls from a container to a model, a tool or the internet all fail; side effects cannot bypass the governed exits & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  3 & Tool-call audit relies on tool-gateway events and can be compared with container self-reports & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  4 & The enterprise-side ledger is append-only, its hash chain verifies, and tampering with a historical event is detected & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  5 & Every run has enforceable hard ceilings on steps, calls, model usage, duration and spend & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  6 & A platform token and a run token used across interfaces are both rejected & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  7 & A forged delegation fails verification against the identity provider & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  8 & With the vendor's API simulated as unavailable, the first two kill-switch stages still take effect & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  9 & Sequence numbers are assigned by the platform and a client resumes from them after a disconnect & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  10 & Administrative actions and agent actions can be replayed in one identity chain & $\mdlgwhtsquare$\\
  \bottomrule
\end{longtable}
